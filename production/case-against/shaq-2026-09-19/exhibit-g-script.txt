Exhibit G: Under Fire Again.

On September seventeenth, FBI Director Kash Patel named Shaquille O'Neal an honorary FBI Special Agent during the National Academy's Session 299 graduation in Quantico. The title is ceremonial, but the moment immediately became part of a larger online argument because of Shaq's long association with law enforcement and Patel's own political profile.

Then the story grew. After a creator criticized the honorary badge, Shaq responded in the comments, telling the critic that he did not have a real job, lived with his mother, was chasing clicks, and that his page sucked.

The prosecution says the episode fits a familiar pattern: Shaq's personality can turn a headline into an even bigger headline. The defense says none of this changes what happened on a basketball court, and a ceremonial honor decades after retirement should not be retroactively used to judge his playing career.

So the jury gets one final question: does this controversy belong in the case against Shaq at all, or is it simply the latest chapter in a public life that has always been bigger than basketball?
